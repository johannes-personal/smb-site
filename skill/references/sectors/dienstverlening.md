# Sector pack: dienstverlening op afspraak

Hairdressers, beauty salons, garages, plumbers, electricians, gardeners,
physiotherapists, dentists, driving schools, and similar appointment-based
trades and practices.

## What the visitor is deciding
"Will these people solve my problem, can I get in soon, and roughly what will
it cost?" Trust and availability outrank everything. For urgent trades (a
leaking pipe, a broken boiler) the entire site is a phone number with
supporting evidence — treat it that way.

## Facts that matter here
- Services offered, with indicative prices or an honest price range. Refusing
  to indicate price at all is the single most common complaint about these
  sites
- Availability: opening hours, appointment-only status, emergency callout, and
  typical waiting time
- Service area — which towns and postcodes. Real decision factor for trades
- Qualifications, registrations, guarantees, insurance
- Real reviews, attributable

## Page structure
1. `Hero` — the problem solved, and the phone number visible without scrolling
2. `QuickLinks` — the top three or four services
3. `CardGrid` — services with indicative pricing
4. `FeatureRow` — why them: years, qualifications, guarantee
5. `Testimonials` — real ones only
6. `MapContact` — service area, hours, phone
7. `FAQ` — the questions they answer on the phone every week
8. `CTABand`

Other pages: one per major service where it justifies its own page (this is
where these businesses win search), about, contact.

## Primary action
Phone, almost always. A `tel:` link, prominent, repeated, and working on mobile.
Only add a form if the business genuinely prefers email. Where they already use
a booking system (common for salons and clinics), embed it.

## Content that earns its place
Indicative prices, service area, response time, qualifications, before-and-after
photos of real work, and the answers to the questions they are actually asked.

## Filler to avoid
"Kwaliteit en service staan bij ons voorop", stock photographs of models with
perfect hair or actors in clean overalls, and a services list so generic it
could belong to any competitor.

## Traps
- Prices deliberately absent because the business fears comparison. Push for a
  range or a starting price; the alternative is losing the visitor entirely
- Regulated professions have claim restrictions — do not write marketing copy
  about medical or dental outcomes without the practitioner's approval
- "Hours" that are really "by appointment". Model that honestly
- Reviews copied from Google without attribution
- Emergency callout rates hidden until the invoice
