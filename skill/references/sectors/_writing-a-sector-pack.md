# Writing a sector pack

A sector pack encodes a point of view about a kind of business. It exists to
stop the design phase from averaging four competitor sites into something
generic, and to tell Phase 3 which facts actually matter here.

Research it once, reuse it across every client in that sector. Keep it under
100 lines — it is a briefing, not a manual.

## Required sections

### 1. What the visitor is deciding
The specific decision, and what they need in order to make it. This is the most
important part of the pack. For a restaurant it is "is this right for tonight,
and can I get a table". For a garage it is "will they fix my problem, when, and
roughly what will it cost".

### 2. Facts that matter here
Which of the standard facts are load-bearing for this sector, plus any
sector-specific ones. This drives Phase 3 far more than the section list does.

### 3. Page structure
Recommended pages and, for the homepage, the section order with reasoning.

### 4. Primary action
What the site should cause. Phone, book, order, visit, request a quote. Note
how it should be repeated and whether an existing external system already
handles it.

### 5. Content that earns its place / filler to avoid
Be specific and opinionated. Every sector has content that every site copies
from every other and that nobody reads.

### 6. Sector-specific traps
The things that go wrong in this sector: seasonal drift, price lists that live
in a third-party system, regulated claims, hours that are really "by
appointment".

## What not to put in a pack

Named competitors' copy, imagery or distinctive layouts. Take the structural
lesson and write original work.
